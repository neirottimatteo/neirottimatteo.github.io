Kleriox

Co-founder & CEO Since September 2025 Turin, Italy kleriox.com ↗

Kleriox exists for one reason: to make complex things clear. Good financial information already exists — it is public, it is free, and it is buried in two-hundred-page documents written by lawyers for other lawyers. The problem is not access. It is reading time.

Fira — intelligence on US stocks

The name is an acronym: Financial Intelligence & Research Assistant. Fira watches the official documents US-listed companies file with the SEC — quarterly results, 8-Ks, annual reports, insider filings — and turns them into short, plain-English reports and alerts, minutes after publication.

The rule that decides everything: no citation, no claim

Fira's most important design constraint is not a model or an architecture. It is that every statement in a report must be anchored to a verifiable passage in the original document. Each generated sentence carries a citation marker; tapping it opens a side panel with the verbatim source excerpt.

It is not a cosmetic feature. It is the difference between a financial tool and a plausibility generator: a language model summarising documents without grounding produces perfectly convincing, occasionally false prose — which is exactly the worst case when someone is deciding where to put money.

How it is built

Monitoring
Three separate polling loops: news and insider filings (SEC Form 4) hourly, 10-Q and 8-K filings from EDGAR every twelve hours, plus the report generation cycles
Backend
FastAPI (Python), Postgres, JWT auth, push and email notification
Frontend
React 19 + Vite, mobile-first, native apps on iOS and Android
Generation
Markdown reports strictly grounded in the document, with source excerpts stored alongside the text
Constraint
The entire MVP was designed to run on a Raspberry Pi 5 — a deliberate limit, not a compromise

The Raspberry Pi deserves a note. Forcing the MVP onto a hundred-euro computer was a strategic choice: it makes you severe about what gets queried, how often, and what it costs. A product that survives a tight constraint scales upward without surprises; one built without constraints discovers its costs on launch day.

The product changed name twice before landing on the right one — internally "Delegate Stock", then "ClariX", both abandoned in July 2026 over trademark conflicts. The settled public name is Fira, under the Kleriox company brand. If you came across DelegateAI in my older posts, it is the same thing.

Libella — the second product

The second Kleriox product is already in production: Libella, a private, on-premise "digital workforce" for an architecture studio. AI agents handle administrative triage, technical research over the studio's own documents, and document generation — with a human approving everything that leaves the building.

Built on FastAPI, Postgres with pgvector for semantic retrieval, and LiteLLM as the single choke point for every model call — which makes usage metering and per-agent routing a single line of config. The tools each agent can use are typed, and the permission model (read / propose / write) is enforced at the tool layer rather than in the prompt. Proposed actions land in an approval queue where a person reviews, edits and executes them.

It is a less flashy product than Fira, but it touches the same nerve: professionals buried under documents nobody has time to read.

The research arm

Alongside the products, Kleriox publishes macroeconomic research. The main piece is the Recession Probability Engine: a model that estimates the probability of a US recession every month and publishes both the number and its full driver-by-driver decomposition in the open.

It is not marketing dressed as research. It is the most direct way I know to show how we think about financial data: by showing the reasoning, month after month, even when the result is boring.
