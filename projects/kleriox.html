Kleriox

Co-founder & CEO Turin, Italy kleriox.com ↗

Live on iOS, Android and kleriox.com since 27 October 2026.

Kleriox is the company I co-founded in 2025 and run as CEO. Our mission is to help everyone invest knowing what they own. Our first MVP is an app that reads the documents US-listed companies file with the SEC and explains them in plain language. It has been live on iOS and Android since 27 October 2026.

Where it comes from

It started with a need of my own. I invest, and I wanted to understand what I own: why a stock moved, whether a bad quarter really mattered, what the company had actually said. The answer was in the documents the company files with the SEC, but I kept missing when they came out, and when I did find one, an SEC document was too hard to read. What I read instead was other people's summaries. The app is how I solved it for myself.

The problem, for more people than me

I do not think I am the only one. Opening an account takes minutes today, and a few euros buy a piece of a share, but understanding has not kept up. Company results and official documents are still written for professionals. Many people stay out. Many of those who are in do not know why their stocks move or whether a bad day matters. And many hand their savings to an advisor or a bank without knowing what they own or what it costs, and fees can take a large share of the gains.

Good advice has its place. I think delegating should be something people choose once they understand what they own.

What the app does

The app follows the filings and the news for the companies you care about. When something new comes out, it writes a short report in plain language a few minutes after publication. Every sentence in the report is linked to the passage of the original document it comes from: tap it and you see the exact text. AI does the reading, and we never tell anyone what to buy or sell.

The link to the source is the most important rule in the project. A language model that summarises without being tied to the document writes convincing sentences that are sometimes false, and in finance that is the worst case. So a claim without a source does not get into a report.

Mission and vision

Our mission is to help everyone invest knowing what they own.

I want stock markets to be easy to understand. Nobody should need a shelf of finance books to know what they own, and the market's gains should not go only to the people who already speak its language. Investing is also a way to use your money: you choose the companies you back, for their prospects and for what they stand for, with confidence, for yourself and your family.

How we work

  • We explain, you decide. What happened, why, and how much it matters. Never what to buy or sell.
  • AI does the reading. It handles the hard parts, and the judgment stays with the person.
  • Simple, without dumbing it down. Every figure traces back to official documents and every term is explained where it appears, so people learn a little finance each time.
  • Honest about worry. We say clearly when something matters, and just as clearly when it does not.
  • In your language. The app is available in eight languages, because understanding your money should not depend on reading English.
  • One incentive. We want people to understand. We do not sell ads, and we never earn from how much people trade.

Where we want to go

We want Kleriox to become one complete tool for investing: everything a person needs to invest, in the simplest way possible, with the information they need to decide. Today it covers one part of that, understanding the companies you own, and we widen from there.

What we got wrong

We built the MVP without validating anything first. Before writing code, we did not check whether anyone other than me wanted this, or whether they would pay for it. A startup is usually advised to do it the other way round: find out whether the problem is real for other people, then build the smallest thing that tests it. With CRONO the request came first. With Kleriox the starting point was a need of my own, and I started building right away.

It is my first real startup project, and I am learning this part by doing it. The good side is that the product exists and works, so we have something to validate with. We will use the live app to find out whether other people want it and whether they would pay for it.

We are building it like a startup, small and fast, though not necessarily for huge scale. Scaling could become a problem later, but it is not the question for now. The app is new, so I have no numbers to show.

How it is built

Monitoring
Three separate polling loops: news and insider filings (SEC Form 4) hourly, 10-Q and 8-K filings from EDGAR every twelve hours, plus the report generation cycles
Backend
FastAPI (Python), Postgres, JWT auth, push and email notification
Frontend
React 19 + Vite, mobile-first, apps on iOS and Android
Generation
Markdown reports grounded in the document, with source excerpts stored alongside the text
Constraint
The entire MVP was designed to run on a Raspberry Pi 5

I chose the Raspberry Pi on purpose: it forces me to be strict about what gets queried, how often, and what it costs.

The research

Alongside the product we publish macroeconomic research. The main piece is the Recession Probability Engine, a model that estimates the probability of a US recession every month and publishes both the number and the breakdown by indicator. There is also a paper.

  • Research and models: the macro engine, from the data pipeline to publication.
  • Strategy: positioning, pricing and partners.
