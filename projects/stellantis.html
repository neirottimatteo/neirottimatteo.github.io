Crash-test data on ISO 13499

Project owner · Test Data Management & Integration Stellantis Safety Center, Orbassano · ALTEN Italia consultant January 2023 — September 2025

At the Stellantis Safety Center in Orbassano I led the project to bring the lab's crash-test data onto ISO 13499, the international standard for exchanging crash-test data.

My role

I worked at the Center from January 2023 to September 2025 as an ALTEN Italia consultant, in Test Data Management & Integration. I was responsible for the lab's crash-test data: accuracy, traceability and compliance with the Center's ISO 17025 accreditation. The ISO 13499 project was the largest part of that job.

The problem

When I arrived, the test data was not standardised. Each acquisition system wrote it in its own way, channel names followed internal conventions built up over the years, and every analysis tool needed its own adapter. Sharing data with other centers was difficult.

ISO 13499 defines how a test is stored for exchange: the measurement channels, the metadata about the test, vehicle, occupants and instrumentation, the multimedia files, and an index that links everything.

How it started

I identified the problem, defined an approach and presented it to my managers. It was a real need of the Center, and I was given the lead on the project. It had not been assigned to me: I proposed it.

The work

I was the project owner. Most of the software was developed by others. My role was to gather the requirements of each group, translate the technical needs into clear requests for the developers, and follow the work through to daily use, keeping an overview so that nothing was lost between groups.

The project covered the whole chain of the lab: the electronics of the acquisition systems, the recording software, the sensor database, test preparation, the test itself, and the export and analysis of the data. The inputs had to be merged into a single one and adapted to the standard, and the output had to be formatted correctly.

This involved many decisions, both on how the system would work for its users and on technical details: converting from g to metres per second squared, sensors with special characteristics, small changes to the software to make daily work easier.

I gathered feedback from the groups involved: the people who run the tests, and those who manage the sensor database, the acquisition hardware and the analysis tools. The aim was a solution that worked for everyone and made the work easier. Their processes were already changing, so I tried not to add difficulty. Ease of use was a requirement.

I also wrote the project documentation, including what was needed to stay compliant with ISO 17025. It was a substantial amount of work.

Other work

For other parts of my job I built Python tools that removed manual, repetitive steps, for example automatic checks on the data before it left the lab and preparing data for new analysis tools. I wrote the first versions myself, and later used AI to improve them. I also read and analysed the compliance reports and gave some technical support when something broke.

Where it ended up

When I left in September 2025, a good share of the tests were being run in ISO 13499 and the tools were in daily use.

I am proud of this work. It involved every department I worked with and a large part of the Center. I am proud of the technical side too, even though I did not develop it myself: integrating acquisition systems, software and the database was not easy, and my role was to understand what each one needed and communicate it clearly to the developers.
