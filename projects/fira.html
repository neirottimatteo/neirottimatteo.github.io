Fira

Co-founder & CEO, Kleriox Turin, Italy kleriox.com ↗

In closed testing on iOS and Android. Public launch planned for autumn 2026.

Fira is the app; Kleriox is the company I co-founded in 2025 and run as CEO. The name is an acronym: Financial Intelligence & Research Assistant. The app watches the official documents US-listed companies file with the SEC — quarterly results, 8-Ks, annual reports, insider filings — and rewrites them in plain language minutes after publication.

Where it comes from

Good financial information already exists and is public. An SEC filing is mandatory, audited and legally binding: the most reliable source there is on a listed company. The problem is not access, it is reading time. Two hundred pages written by lawyers for other lawyers, four times a year, for every company you follow.

Almost everything you read elsewhere — an article, a post, a comment — is a second-hand reading of that document, filtered through someone with an agenda and a few hours late. Fira shortens that chain: it compresses the original document instead of commenting on it.

The rule that decides everything: no citation, no claim

The most important constraint in the project is not a model or an architecture: it is that every statement in a report must be tied to a verifiable passage in the original document. Each generated sentence carries a citation marker, and tapping it opens a panel with the verbatim source excerpt.

A language model summarising documents without grounding produces convincing prose that is occasionally false. In finance that is the worst case: someone decides where to put money on the strength of a sentence nobody can trace back to a document.

How it is built

Monitoring
Three separate polling loops: news and insider filings (SEC Form 4) hourly, 10-Q and 8-K filings from EDGAR every twelve hours, plus the report generation cycles
Backend
FastAPI (Python), Postgres, JWT auth, push and email notification
Frontend
React 19 + Vite, mobile-first, apps on iOS and Android
Generation
Markdown reports grounded in the document, with source excerpts stored alongside the text
Constraint
The entire MVP was designed to run on a Raspberry Pi 5

The Raspberry Pi deserves a note. Forcing the MVP onto a hundred-euro computer was a deliberate choice: it makes you severe about what gets queried, how often, and what it costs. A product that survives a tight constraint scales upward without surprises on the cost side.

The product changed name twice before landing on the right one — internally "Delegate Stock", then "ClariX", both dropped in July 2026 over trademark conflicts. If you came across DelegateAI in my older posts, it is the same thing.

The research

Alongside the product we publish macroeconomic research. The main piece is the Recession Probability Engine: a model that estimates the probability of a US recession every month and publishes both the number and the drivers that moved it. There is a paper too.

My role

  • Product direction — what we build, what we leave out, and in what order.
  • Architecture — the structural choices, from the Raspberry Pi constraint to how reports stay tied to their source.
  • Research and modelling — the macro engine, from data pipeline to publication.
  • Strategy — positioning, pricing, investor and partner relationships.

Kleriox is not a full-time occupation: I run it alongside university, which is still the priority.
