Recession Probability Engine

A monthly probability of a US recession, built on 58 features drawn from around sixty FRED series with history back to 1939. 0.959 average out-of-sample AUC and 13.2 months of advance warning. Every prediction is decomposed with SHAP. There is a paper, published on GitHub.

About

The short version

Diploma in electronics and automation at ITI Ettore Majorana with 100/100. At twenty I was at the Stellantis Safety Center in Orbassano, where for almost three years I worked on crash-test data. In September 2025 I left to enrol at the Politecnico di Torino, which is now my main occupation.

Kleriox started around the same time, with the Fira app, and I run it alongside my studies. It is not a full-time company — it is what I work on when I am not on an exam.

There is also Libella, an AI assistant built for an architecture studio, in production since 2026. It is a smaller thing and I do not write much about it.

Economics

Economics started as curiosity and took over most of what I do. I follow US macro fairly stubbornly, and the two projects I have spent the most time on in the past two years — the recession model and Fira — both come from the same place: public economic data is already good, and the hard part is reading it.

What interests me most is where economics becomes measurable: what actually leads a cycle, which indicators hold up out of sample, how much of a quarterly filing matters and how much is noise.

AI

I use AI every day and in depth — as a power user, not as a curiosity. I write code, read technical documents, test ideas and build pipelines with these tools inside the process rather than beside it. Several of the projects on this site were built partly with AI help, and I would rather say so than pretend otherwise.

There is also an ongoing experiment on applying machine learning to a new area. It is too early to write about it.
